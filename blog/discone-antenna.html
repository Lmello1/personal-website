Flat-Pack 3D Printed Discone Antenna

Published: 8/15/2026


The finished antenna on it's tripod mount


For one of my projects, DART, I needed an affordable, small form factor, and extremely wideband antenna. I was attracted to the inherent wide band characteristics of discone antennas but was discouraged by the high unit price for COTS units. As I was looking into alternatives I could not shake the idea of the possibility of making an extremely inexpensive discone antenna. One of the main price drivers for commercial discones is the use of a complex threaded main hub that all the radials screw into. While this is great for portability and ease of assembly, complicated metal parts like that do no favors for affordability.


COTS complex centeral hub


I decided to attempt a design that utilized 3D printing and sheet metal for the central hub, cutting down the cost drastically to around 10 dollars per unit while still maintaining great wideband reception characteristics. Targeting a frequency range of 100Mhz to 1.7Ghz to allow for reception across almost the entire RTL-SDR reception range, dimensions for the radials were calculated. The top disk is a simple design, just 8 radials contacting the upper sheet metal plate, the lower cone is mildly more complicated, using bent radials.


A bulkhead SMA mount is threaded into the bottom sheet metal plate, connecting to the cone radials, than the center core is soldered to the top sheet metal, connecting to the disk radials. Everything is than sandwiched together and held in compression by 4 M3 socket head cap screws through the top. This simple design makes it easy to deploy and take down in the field. Another advantage to this design is that all the radials can be stored within the PVC pipe and packed inside the tripod bag!

NANOvna Preformance | "Not great, not terrable"


Performance wise for the price it is not bad! There are some nasty resonances that appear closer to the frequency limit (anything above 1.2Ghz or so) If people are interested, I have the full cut list, BOM, and all the 3D models available HERE on github to make this at home! I am sure there is a lot of room for improvement (off the top of my head, I am sure improving the contact area between the radials and the sheet metal would do wonders for RF performance) so forks and comments are very much appreciated!
